A Wurlitzer 200A,
rebuilt in fixed point.

Reed is a from-scratch firmware project for the M-VAVE FM-1. The voice is a fixed-point port of OpenWurli's modal reed, electrostatic pickup and hammer models. No samples, no impulse responses: seven reeds modes, a field-solved pickup law and the 200A's amplifier character, running on a 32-bit JieLi AC79.

What is inside

01 / REED

Seven-mode reed

Euler–Bernoulli mode ratios with tip mass, per-note inharmonicity, damping and the velocity-dependent hammer dwell. Quadrature oscillators, no per-sample sines.

02 / PICKUP

Electrostatic pickup

A 2D field-solved capacitance-vs-overlap law, one moving reed against a fixed plate node. The nonlinearity is in the source, exactly as in the 200A.

03 / HAMMER

Hammer and damper

Gaussian contact dwell, register-dependent onset, attack noise and the progressive felt damper. High modes die first.

04 / AMP

200A character

Input coupling, preamp asymmetry, Twin-T/LDR tremolo, volume pot, feedback shelf, crossover distortion and the 4×8″ speaker. Calibrated against OpenWurli.

05 / FIXED POINT

No FPU required

Q30 reed, Q24 gain staging, Q28 biquads, 32×32→64 MACs. The same C99 source builds for the AC79 and for WebAssembly, bit-identically.

06 / HARDWARE

FM-1, not a computer

The target is a 360 MHz pi32v2 with 8 voices by default (32-slot capacity), the FM-1 keybed, encoders and LCD. The firmware layer builds on Felucca's BSP; the emulator is the reference.

Signal path

7-mode reed→ pickup overlap law→ hammer / damper→ preamp→ LDR tremolo→ volume pot→ power amp→ speaker→ Q20 out

The voice path is a direct fixed-point port of OpenWurli's physics. The amplifier and speaker are a calibrated behavioural model in v0.1; the exact circuit solver is the next milestone. See the porting notes for what is exact and what is approximated.
